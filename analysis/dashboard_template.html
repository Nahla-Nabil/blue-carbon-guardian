Case study: five conversions inside mangrove polygons, confirmed on sub-metre imagery

Sentinel-2 true colour, same stand outline in yellow.

Stand 5 — 11 Jan 2021 → 7 May 2026

A large coastal reclamation with finger canals appeared between the 2021 and 2023 images. Dating the change from the affected pixels (778 Sentinel-2 dates) puts its start in Feb–Apr 2023. Our alert was already on from 25 Oct 2022, mostly from cells that later converted (18 of 23), and stayed on through the conversion. The stand's tide-corrected NDVI fell from 0.27 to below zero. The eastern part of the outline is still mangrove.

Stand 9 — 23 Sep 2020 → 7 May 2026

A canal-style coastal development (sand islands, lagoons) now covers the south-eastern part of the outline; the north-western, denser mangrove part is unchanged. Stand 9 had the most alert episodes of any stand (7) and was independently flagged by the epoch-difference indicator (17.5 % of its area). Its stand average still reads “stable”, but 8 of its 28 cells (21.8 ha) show severe decline, exactly the converted part. Dated from the affected pixels, the change started in Jun 2023. Our alert was already on from 7 Dec 2022, raised by cells in the south-east part that later converted.

Stand 12 — found only by the 200 m cells

The stand average still reads “stable”, but 6 of its 20 cells (13.2 ha) show severe decline along its southern edge. Summer images (flagged cells in red) show why: intact until Aug 2022; by Sep 2023 a new canal crosses the stand and reclamation lagoons press against its edge; by 2025–26 construction reaches into the flagged cells. Our alert first fired on 17 Dec 2022, between the last intact image and the first converted one. The same development later reached stand 18 (converted in 2025, dated start Feb 2025): our alert was on from 29 Oct 2024 across much of the stand (not specific to the part that converted) and again from 17 Apr 2025, from the converting cells. At stand 6, sparse mangrove on a tidal flat: new canals encircle it by Feb 2023 and part of it is a lagoon by Jan 2025. Our alert (from its cells) began on 17 Feb 2023. Stands 12, 18 and 6 were found only by the 200 m cells.

Checked at 0.3–0.5 m: dated WorldView-2/3 and Legion-1 captures (Esri World Imagery Wayback) confirm all five conversions. At each site our alert began between the last undisturbed capture and the first capture showing works (stand 5: Mar 2022 → Feb 2023, alert Oct 2022; stand 9: works next to it by Feb 2023, alert Dec 2022; stand 12: Mar 2022 → Feb 2023, alert Dec 2022; stand 18: Nov 2023 → Jan 2025, alert Oct 2024; stand 6: Mar 2022 → Feb 2023, alert Feb 2023). This is detection of works as they happen, not advance warning. Change dates come from the pixels and images themselves, not from permits or construction records. The outlines come from a 2021 land-cover map and include ground that is now provably non-mangrove: all these losses are partial and their carbon figures are upper bounds.

Independent hyperspectral check (EnMAP, 224 bands)

Agreement with our Sentinel-2 series: on the same date, EnMAP-derived indices match our stand medians (r = 0.84–0.97 across 25 stands on 1 Nov 2022; 0.97–0.99 across 14 stands on 14 Apr 2025).

Stand 5: 685 of 1,518 mangrove-classified 30 m pixels (about 62 ha, 45 %) changed from a vegetation spectrum to a bright, mineral-like one (sand or construction material). This brackets our conservative Sentinel-2 flag (26 ha): the loss is 26–62 ha by two independent methods.

What hyperspectral did not show: a better mangrove classifier (F1 0.74 vs 0.72 for Sentinel-2-like bands, within noise) or early stress. Two dates in different seasons cannot separate stress from season and tide.

How to read it, and what we can and cannot claim

Two signals

Alert: the last five observations are unusually low compared with a model of the stand's own season, trend and tide state, either for the stand average (−1.5 σ) or for any one of its 200 m cells (−3.5 σ). The two levels are set together so that false alarms stay at about one episode per stand-year (0.96 measured).

Condition: the median tide-corrected index of the last 12 months (a full seasonal cycle) compared with the stand's 2020–21 median. A sustained decline is not forgotten.

200 m cells: the same two signals per 4 ha block. Against an independent change method (2021 vs 2025 composites), cells flagged 11 of 15 changed blocks and 11 of 554 unchanged ones; most of those 11 are real conversions that the stricter method misses. On 65 stands the rule never saw (Abu Dhabi and Saudi Arabia's Tarut Bay), with its thresholds fixed, the combined alert gave 0.79 episodes per stand-year, against 0.96 where it was calibrated.

Tested performance (simulation)

We injected artificial canopy loss into the real series of stands with no known change, with false alarms held to about one per stand-year. A detection counts only if a new alert starts after the loss begins, and every rate is shown next to the rate by chance (the same test with no loss).

With the combined alert (stand average OR any 200 m cell), a partial loss (a tenth of a stand losing half its canopy) is caught within 60 days in about 73 % of cases (13 % by chance). A uniform 10 % loss over a whole stand is harder: about 63 % within 6 months (36 % by chance). Tide correction alone cut noise by 30–35 %.

This corrects our earlier figures (58 % / 90 %): that test also counted alerts already running and had no chance baseline. The alerts shown in this dashboard use the combined rule.

Limits

Simulated losses are cover loss, not leaf stress. The stand outlines come from a 2021 map that our reference check found imperfect (about 65 % agreement on confident points). Reference labels are AI-assisted photo-interpretation and cover extent, not health. Carbon uses 2013–14 field plots from four nearby sites, so the range is wide and excludes soil below 1 m.
